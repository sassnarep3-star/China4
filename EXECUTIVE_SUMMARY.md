# Executive summary — getting money out of China as a US citizen

**Project:** China4 · **Prepared:** 8–9 November 2026 · **Method:** every figure traced to an official source; anything unconfirmed is left blank and flagged, never inferred.

---

## 1. The bottom line

**There is no tax in China and no tax in the US on the act of converting CNY to USD and wiring your own money to your own US account.** The cost you are worried about has three separate parts, and only one of them is a real, unavoidable cost:

| Part | What it is | Typical size | Avoidable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bank charges + FX spread** | The bank's exchange margin and wire fees | **0.21% – 0.54%** of the amount | Only by shopping providers. Largely unavoidable, and small. |
| **Chinese tax** | Tax already owed on how the money was *earned* | Unknown — depends on your facts | Avoidable only if the income was already taxed in China and you can prove it |
| **Penalties** | Fines for illegal channels, or US reporting penalties | **30% – 300%** of the amount (illegal channels); up to **$16,536** non-willful per FBAR violation | **Entirely avoidable. Stay legal and file the reports.** |

The cheapest legal route costs **well under 1%**. The illegal route costs **30% to 300%** and can cost you the whole principal. That is the entire trade-off.

## 2. The single most important finding

**Chinese foreign-exchange rules classify your transaction by the *source of the funds*, not by your passport.** Before any method can be chosen, you must establish:

1. **How the money was earned** — salary, business income, property sale, gift, inheritance, or savings.
2. **Whether Chinese tax was paid on it** and whether you hold a tax certificate (纳税证明).
3. **Your Chinese FX residency status** — whether you hold, or ever held, a Chinese identity document or a foreign permanent-resident ID card.
4. **Whether the income was ever reported to the IRS.**

Everything below is conditional on those four answers.

## 5. US tax and reporting — what you must not skip

Moving your own money to the US is **not** a taxable event. But these filings are mandatory, and the penalties apply **whether or not any tax is owed**:

| Form | Trigger | Penalty if missed |
|---|---|---|
| **FBAR** (FinCEN Form 114) | Foreign accounts exceeded **US$10,000 in aggregate** at any time in the year | Non-willful up to **$16,536** per violation; willful the greater of **$165,353** or **50% of the account balance** — [31 CFR 1010.821](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-H/section-1010.821) |
| **Form 8938** (FATCA) | Specified foreign financial assets above the threshold (from US$50,000 for a single US resident) | $10,000 per failure, up to $50,000 on continued failure after IRS notice |
| **Form 3520** Part IV | Gifts over **US$100,000** from a foreign individual or estate; **US$20,573** (2026) from foreign corporations/partnerships | 5% of the gift per month, capped at 25% — [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/gifts-from-foreign-person) |
| **FinCEN Form 105** | Bringing **more than US$10,000** in monetary instruments into the US | Seizure; civil and criminal penalties |

Two nuances worth taking to an adviser: **converting a currency is itself a disposal** — under [IRS section 988 guidance](https://www.irs.gov/pub/fatca/int_practice_units/int_c_041.pdf) a nonfunctional-currency disposition can produce a foreign-currency gain or loss. And the **foreign earned income exclusion is $132,900 for 2026** ([Rev. Proc. 2025-32](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf)), with the foreign tax credit on Form 1116 as the main relief against double taxation.

## 6. What to do, in order

1. **Classify the money.** Source, tax paid, your Chinese FX status, IRS history. Nothing else can be decided until this is done.
2. **Ask the branch, in writing:** which category applies, which documents it needs, the executable rate, and the all-in fees including intermediary and receiving-bank charges.
3. **Compare net USD received**, not the displayed rate. Use [`estimates.html`](estimates.html) for providers and [`costs.html`](costs.html) for routes.
4. **Wire it once** if you can document the source — the sending charge is capped, so a single large wire is cheaper per dollar than many small ones.
5. **Keep everything:** conversion slip, tax certificate, remittance advice, and the bank's written quote.
6. **File** FBAR, Form 8938 and (if applicable) Form 3520 on time. If you are behind, look at the [IRS Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/streamlined-filing-compliance-procedures) before the IRS contacts you.
7. **Never** use underground changers, crypto P2P, or borrowed quotas.

## 7. Flagged for review

Published on the **[Flagged for review](flagged.html)** page with source links. The ones that matter most:

- **Rate anomaly.** On the 9 Nov 2026 snapshot, BOC's published USD selling rate (6.6929) sat *below* the PBOC central parity (6.7168) — the opposite of the usual relationship. Both cost pages therefore use the internally consistent half-spread of BOC's own buy/sell quotes rather than a comparison with parity. Verify both rates on the day.
- **The rate board is not a quote.** Bank of China states on its own board that it is for reference and the executed rate governs.
- **Does a US passport alone give you a US$50,000 quota?** Article 2 of the SAFE Detailed Rules covers purchases by *domestic* individuals; SAFE separately extends the quota to holders of a foreign permanent-resident ID card. A US passport by itself is not shown by the verified pages to create a quota. **Confirm with the bank.**
- **A previously cited fine no longer exists.** SAFE Detailed Rules Article 39 (fine up to CNY 1,000 on individuals) was repealed by SAFE circular 汇发〔2023〕8号.
- **Conflict in the property-transfer threshold.** Article 10 says "CNY 500,000 or less" goes to the local branch; SAFE's own release says "below CNY 500,000". The boundary case is ambiguous.
- **Emigration/inheritance transfer eligibility** does not on its face cover a US citizen's own savings.
- **Intermediary and US receiving-bank fees** are blank by policy — no official published figure exists.
- **Provider FX spreads at CCB, HSBC China, ICBC and Western Union China** remain unverified on `estimates.html` rather than extrapolated from BOC.

## 8. Not advice

This is research compiled from official sources, not a bank quote and not a determination of your eligibility. Rules change, bank practice varies, and your facts decide what is legal for you. Verify current rates and your documents with the bank and with a qualified US–China cross-border tax adviser before moving money.
