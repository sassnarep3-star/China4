The short answer

There is no single cheap trick. The lawful route depends on two facts about you, and the real cost is the bank's exchange-rate spread, not its fees.

  1. First, work out which rule set applies to you. Chinese law splits individuals into domestic (境内个人) and non-domestic (境外个人). Under State Council Order 532 Art. 52(2), a foreigner who has lived in China continuously for one full year counts as domestic. Below that you are non-domestic. This decides whether the famous USD 50,000 annual ceiling binds you at all. S02 S01
  2. If the money is documented salary or other current-account income and you are non-domestic, convert at the bank under Detailed Rules Art. 13 with your ID, proof of the amount and your Chinese tax vouchers. That article states no annual numeric cap. Then wire it out under Art. 15, which for a non-domestic individual's FX savings account requires only ID. S01
  3. If you are domestic (including a US citizen resident a year or more), you get USD 50,000 a year on ID alone; above that you need documents proving a genuine current-account transaction. Do not borrow relatives' quotas — that is quota splitting and it puts you on a three-year watch list. S01 S03
  4. If it is a large capital balance — an inheritance, or assets you owned before emigrating — the quota routes will never be enough. Use the purpose-built inheritance transfer or emigration transfer channel, which has no annual ceiling but needs SAFE approval and a tax certificate. S08 S09 S10
  5. On the US side, moving your own money is not income — but converting RMB can produce an ordinary currency gain under IRC 988, and the FBAR and Form 8938 duties apply regardless of whether any tax is due. The reporting penalties are the expensive part. S19 S15 S16

Biggest avoidable cost: the exchange-rate spread. Bank of China charges no separate fee for converting at its own branches, which makes conversion look free. It is not — the bank earns the gap between its quoted rate and the interbank rate. No official spread figure is published, so this site leaves it blank and shows you how to measure it yourself. Three things that will cost you money if you get them wrong

The USD 50,000 myth

It limits purchase of foreign currency by domestic individuals. It does not apply to a non-domestic individual converting documented lawful income under Art. 13.

S01 S02

The fine is up to 100%, not 500%

Order 532 Arts. 39, 40 and 45 all say up to 30%, and in serious cases 30% up to 100% of the amount. Earlier versions of this site claimed 300% and 500%. Those figures do not exist.

S02

Bank fees change in three days

From 2026-11-11 Bank of China raises Mastercard and VISA overseas ATM fees to 2.5%, capped at USD 40 — up from 0.5% capped at USD 5. UnionPay is unchanged.
