Getting money out of China — CNY → USD for a US citizen

Every number and rule on this site is traced to an official source that was retrieved and read on 2026-11-07 (SAFE, PBOC, State Council/gov.cn, State Taxation Administration, IRS, FinCEN, CBP, US Code). Unverifiable figures are not shown as fact — they live on the Flagged for Review page.

Executive summary

Bottom line: a bank conversion is not in itself a Chinese income tax; US foreign-currency gains may be taxable. What actually gates a large, legal transfer out of China is documentation. The bank’s question is never “how much do you want to convert?” but “can you prove this RMB is legal income whose tax was paid?” For qualifying current-account income, the cited rules do not set a fixed numerical ceiling, but the bank must review the source and supporting documents. Other categories may follow different rules.

Who you are matters. A US citizen working in China is an 境外个人 (overseas individual) in Chinese FX law. That means the famous US$50,000 annual convenience quota — an ID-only purchase facility — generally does not apply to you (unless you hold a Chinese permanent-residence card). Your channel is both simpler and bigger: the documented after-tax income purchase, confirmed in SAFE’s own Q&As with no upper limit other than your documented taxed income (SAFE art. 59 guidance; 实施细则 art. 13).

The “considerable tax” decoded. What you may be experiencing as a heavy penalty is a stack of four different things, only two of which are taxes at all:

  • ① China IIT on the underlying income — wage income is taxed at progressive 3%–45% brackets (property-transfer gains at a flat 20%). If payroll tax was already withheld, this is a paper step, not a new cost: the bank converts your after-tax income. If the money was never taxed, this is where the real bill appears — a separate tax filing can apply to certain outbound payments; foreign individuals follow personal FX rules.
  • ② Bank charges & exchange-rate spread — wire/cable fees plus the buy-sell spread. Real but vary by provider and transaction; the common published figures could not be verified on an official bank page, so they are flagged, not asserted.
  • ③ US-side: disclosures, not taxes. Moving your own money is not taxable. But while accounts abroad aggregate above $10,000 you file an FBAR; Form 8938 above its thresholds; Form 3520 if family gifts from related foreign persons top $100,000; FinCEN 105 if you physically carry >$10,000 cash. All informational — with sharp penalties only for not filing (statutory FBAR willful penalty: greater of $100,000 or 50% of the balance).
  • ④ The true “penalties” — underground banks, quota splitting (“蚂蚁搬家”) and crypto off-ramps are prohibited or restricted under distinct rules. For qualifying foreign-exchange violations, fines can be up to 30% of the amount, or 30%–100% in serious cases; confiscation and criminal referral may also apply (外汇管理条例 arts 39/45; 银发〔2021〕237号).

Recommended core plan (analysis built on the verified rules — see Conversion Methods for every channel):

  1. Assemble the source-of-funds chain: passport, employment/service contracts, income statements, bank credit records, and above all the 完税证明 (tax payment records) covering the balance.
  2. Convert at a major FX-designated bank via the documented-income channel (no fixed cap), then wire to your own US account. FX already converted moves out at ≤ US$50,000/day with ID, more with the same documents.
  3. For very large or one-time exits (apartment sale, inheritance, post-emigration assets), use the documented asset-sale route or the SAFE-approved emigration/inheritance transfer program.
  4. Use card spending/abroad ATM withdrawals (RMB 100k/yr cash cap) and small cash carriage for living expenses — not for the lump sum.
  5. Fintech (Wise) for medium repeating amounts once income verification is done.
  6. Never touch quota-stacking, underground banks or crypto — the fines scale with your transfer, the exact thing you were trying to avoid.

US checklist after the wire lands: FBAR for the years the China accounts existed (>$10k aggregate); Form 8938 with your 1040 if above threshold; Form 3520 if relatives’ gifts exceed $100,000 in aggregate; foreign tax credit (Form 1116) for eligible China IIT, subject to limitations; keep every 完税证明, conversion slip and wire advice.
