M01 · Convert lawful RMB income to USD as a non-domestic individual (境外个人), then wire Lawful if conditions met

Who it is for
US citizen who has NOT resided continuously in China for a full year
How it works
Art. 13(1): RMB income lawfully earned under the current account in China is converted at the bank on presentation of valid ID plus supporting documents evidencing the transaction amount, including tax payment vouchers. The USD is then remitted under Art. 15.
Limit
No annual quota is imposed on this route by Art. 13. The amount is bounded by the documented lawful income, not by a fixed cap. The USD 50,000 annual total in Art. 2 applies to settlement by individuals and to purchase by DOMESTIC individuals only.
Documents required
Valid ID; documents evidencing the transaction amount; tax payment vouchers (税务凭证)
China-side cost
Bank FX spread plus remittance tariff. Bank of China charges no separate fee for FX exchange at its own branches (S12 item 210107011); remittance tariff at S12 item 210105008.
US tax treatment
Not income merely because it is transferred. But a currency gain on disposition of the RMB can be ordinary income under IRC 988 (S19). FBAR / Form 8938 apply to the account.

Advantages

  • Not subject to the USD 50,000 annual purchase quota, because Art. 2 limits purchase only for domestic individuals
  • Uses the ordinary banking system, so a clean documentary trail exists
  • No Chinese tax arises on the conversion itself; tax was due on the underlying income when earned

Drawbacks

  • Requires tax vouchers proving the income was declared in China; undocumented savings cannot use it
  • Only covers current-account income, not capital such as property sale proceeds
  • Bank must perform authenticity review and may refuse if documents are incomplete

Official sources: S01 S02 S12 S19

M02 · Purchase FX within the USD 50,000 annual total, then wire (domestic individual) Lawful

Who it is for
Chinese citizens, and foreigners who HAVE resided in China continuously for one year (Order 532 Art. 52(2))
How it works
Art. 2: within the annual total, purchase is handled at the bank on valid ID alone. Art. 3: the FX purchased may be remitted abroad, deposited to the person's own FX savings account, or carried out subject to the banknote rules.
Limit
USD 50,000 equivalent per person per year for purchase by a domestic individual (Art. 2). SAFE may adjust the annual total.
Documents required
Valid personal identity document only, while within the annual total
China-side cost
Bank FX spread plus remittance tariff (S12). No Chinese tax on the act of conversion.
US tax treatment
Transfer of own funds is not itself income; IRC 988 currency gain may still arise (S19). FBAR / Form 8938 apply.

Advantages

  • Minimal paperwork inside the quota: ID only
  • Predictable and repeatable each calendar year
  • Cheapest documented channel for amounts at or below the quota

Drawbacks

  • Hard annual ceiling; large balances take multiple years
  • Borrowing or lending quota to move more is expressly policed and leads to watch-listing (S03)
  • A US citizen who has lived in China a year is caught by this cap, which many guides get wrong

Official sources: S01 S02 S03 S12

M03 · Above-quota current-account purchase with transaction documents (domestic individual) Lawful if conditions met

Who it is for
Domestic individuals whose genuine current-account need exceeds USD 50,000
How it works
Art. 2 and Art. 12: above the annual total, a domestic individual purchases FX for non-business current-account purposes at the bank on valid ID plus supporting documents evidencing the transaction amount.
Limit
No fixed ceiling stated; bounded by the documented genuine transaction amount. Art. 12 sets a documentary test, not a numeric cap.
Documents required
Valid ID plus documents evidencing the transaction amount for the current-account purpose
China-side cost
Bank FX spread plus remittance tariff (S12)
US tax treatment
Same analysis as M02

Advantages

  • Lawful way to exceed USD 50,000 in a single year
  • No SAFE pre-approval needed for current-account items handled at the bank

Drawbacks

  • Only for genuine current-account purposes; it is not a route for moving investment capital
  • Bank performs authenticity review under Art. 6 and must reject fabricated transactions
  • False or invalid documents convert this into illegal arbitrage under Order 532 Art. 40

Official sources: S01 S02 S12

M04 · Remit FX already held in your own foreign-currency savings account Lawful

Who it is for
Any individual holding USD or other FX in a Chinese FX savings account
How it works
Art. 15(1): a non-domestic individual remits FX from an FX savings account abroad on valid ID alone. Art. 14: a domestic individual may remit up to USD 50,000 equivalent per day on ID alone; above that, current-account authenticity vouchers are required.
Limit
Non-domestic individual: no numeric cap stated in Art. 15(1) for remitting FX from a savings account. Domestic individual: USD 50,000 equivalent PER DAY on ID alone (Art. 14), above which documents are needed. This daily threshold is separate from the annual purchase quota.
Documents required
Non-domestic: valid ID. Domestic: valid ID up to USD 50,000 per day; authenticity vouchers above.
China-side cost
Remittance tariff only; no conversion needed if the balance is already USD (S12)
US tax treatment
No conversion means no new IRC 988 disposition at this step. FBAR / Form 8938 still apply.

Advantages

  • If the money is already in USD, the FX spread is avoided entirely
  • For a non-domestic individual Art. 15(1) states no numeric ceiling
  • The daily USD 50,000 threshold for domestic individuals is far less restrictive than the annual purchase quota

Drawbacks

  • Only helps if the balance is already in foreign currency
  • Converting RMB into that FX account first still triggers the purchase rules in M01/M02/M03

Official sources: S01 S12

M10 · Bank draft (票汇) and clean collection (光票托收) Lawful if conditions met

Who it is for
Individuals able to visit a branch
How it works
A bank draft is a negotiable instrument carried or mailed abroad and presented for payment. Huifa [2003] No. 102 notice item 3 states Customs no longer controls the carrying of foreign-currency payment instruments and securities. Bank of China publishes tariffs for both cross-border draft remittance and clean collection.
Limit
The underlying FX purchase remains subject to whichever of M01/M02/M03 applies. No separate instrument-specific quota was found.
Documents required
As required for the underlying purchase; draft presentation abroad
China-side cost
Draft remittance: 0.1% of amount, min RMB 50, max RMB 260, plus postage if any. Clean collection: 0.1%, min RMB 50, max RMB 250, plus actual postage; RMB 10 return fee if the overseas bank returns it (S12 items 210105008, 210106010).
US tax treatment
A bearer or unrestrictedly endorsed instrument over USD 10,000 is a monetary instrument for FinCEN 105 (S21).

Advantages

  • Carrying a draft is not subject to the banknote carrying-permit tiers
  • Published, capped tariff

Drawbacks

  • Slow clearing; the paying bank may return the item
  • Still counts toward US monetary-instrument reporting if physically carried
  • Underlying purchase quota is unchanged

Official sources: S04 S12 S21

M16 · Family support and donation receipts above the quota, with documents Lawful if conditions met

Who it is for
Domestic individuals receiving genuine family support or donations from abroad
How it works
Art. 10 lists the documentary basis for above-quota settlement, including notarised donation agreements and family-support evidence such as proof of relationship and the overseas payer's income or tax records.
Limit
No fixed cap stated; bounded by the documented genuine amount
Documents required
Donation: notarised donation agreement or contract. Family support: proof of direct family relationship or notarised support relationship, plus the overseas payer's income evidence such as bank deposit proof or tax vouchers (Art. 10(1)-(2)).
China-side cost
Bank FX spread; notary costs not verified
US tax treatment
Note this is an INBOUND route. A US person receiving a gift from a foreign person above USD 100,000 files Form 3520 Part IV (S17).

Advantages

  • Documented route for genuine cross-border family transfers

Drawbacks

  • Runs in the wrong direction for someone trying to get money OUT of China
  • Fabricating a donation to move funds is illegal arbitrage under Order 532 Art. 40

Official sources: S01 S02 S17

How it works
Art. 9(1): a personal foreign-trade operator conducts trade-related FX purchase, payment, receipt and settlement through their own FX settlement account, with receipts and payments managed as for an institution.
Limit
Managed under institutional rules rather than the individual annual total
Documents required
Business licence or other practice certificate; commerce-authority filing conferring foreign-trade operating rights
China-side cost
—
US tax treatment
Trade income is taxable business income; a US person may face Schedule C and foreign entity reporting.

Advantages

  • Genuine trade flows sit outside the USD 50,000 individual quota

Drawbacks

  • Requires a real registered trading business with real goods flows
  • Using a trade shell with fabricated invoices is expressly penalised (Order 532 Art. 40)

M05 · Inheritance transfer (继承财产转移) — repatriating a Chinese estate you inherited Lawful if conditions met

Who it is for
A foreign citizen who has lawfully inherited an estate located in China
How it works
PBOC [2004] No. 16 Art. 2 and Art. 9: the heir liquidates the inherited domestic estate, applies to SAFE at the decedent's former household-registration locality, then purchases and remits FX through a designated bank. Now administered under Huifa [2024] No. 12 s.6.4.
Limit
No annual quota. Approval threshold: total at or below RMB 500,000 equivalent is approved by the local SAFE branch; above that the branch reviews and forwards to SAFE head office (Art. 10). Estate from one decedent must be applied for in one application but may be remitted in one or several instalments (Art. 6).
Documents required
Written application; FX business application form; identity documents; proof of overseas residence issued or authenticated by a Chinese embassy/consulate; proof of entitlement to the inherited property; the decedent's property title documents; tax certificate or tax payment voucher from the competent tax authority at the property's location; notarisation where required (Art. 9)
China-side cost
Bank FX spread plus remittance tariff. Notary and document costs apply but no official schedule was verified.
US tax treatment
An inheritance is generally not income to the recipient, but Form 3520 Part IV applies above USD 100,000 from a foreign estate (S17). Income earned by the estate and currency gain can still be reportable.

Advantages

  • Purpose-built lawful channel for exactly the 'money stuck in China' problem where the money is inherited
  • No USD 50,000 annual ceiling and no staged-remittance rule for a single decedent's estate
  • Explicitly open to foreign citizens, so US citizenship is not an obstacle

Drawbacks

  • Requires a tax certificate for the decedent's property, which is often the practical bottleneck
  • SAFE will not accept the application if the property is subject to judicial restriction or pending criminal or civil proceedings (Art. 12)
  • Art. 11 forbids taking the proceeds out as banknotes in China; funds must go to the heir's own account abroad
  • Cannot prove lawful source of funds means refusal (Art. 12)

Official sources: S08 S09 S10 S17

M06 · Emigration transfer (移民财产转移) — moving pre-emigration assets out Lawful if conditions met

Who it is for
A natural person who emigrated from mainland China to a foreign country, or settled in Hong Kong, Macao or Taiwan
How it works
PBOC [2004] No. 16 Art. 2 and Art. 8: liquidate lawful property owned in China BEFORE acquiring immigrant status, apply to SAFE at the former household-registration locality, then purchase and remit through a designated bank.
Limit
No annual quota, but staged remittance under Art. 6: first remittance no more than half the approved total; after one year no more than half the remainder; after two years the entire remainder. If the whole approved amount is RMB 200,000 equivalent or less it may be remitted in one lump sum. Art. 10 approval split at RMB 500,000.
Documents required
Written application explaining reason and source of property; applicant information form; FX business application form; Chinese household-registration cancellation certificate issued by public security; proof of overseas settlement issued or authenticated by a Chinese embassy/consulate; property title documents such as title deeds, sale contracts or relocation compensation agreements; notarised agency agreement if applicable (Art. 8)
China-side cost
Bank FX spread plus remittance tariff. Note IIT Art. 10(5) and Art. 13 require tax clearance before household-registration cancellation (S11).
US tax treatment
Transfer of own capital is not itself income. IRC 988 currency gain and FBAR / Form 8938 still apply.

Advantages

  • The only general-purpose lawful channel for moving a large pre-emigration capital balance
  • No USD 50,000 annual ceiling

Drawbacks

  • Requires a Chinese household-registration cancellation certificate, so it is unavailable to a US citizen who never held Chinese hukou
  • Only covers property owned BEFORE immigrant status was acquired
  • Staged remittance can take two years for larger amounts
  • Same judicial-restriction and lawful-source bars as M05 (Art. 12)

Official sources: S08 S09 S10 S11

M25 · Reconvert unused RMB back to FX using the original exchange memo Lawful

Who it is for
A non-domestic individual who previously converted FX into RMB
How it works
Art. 13(2): unused RMB from an earlier conversion is converted back on presentation of valid ID and the original exchange memo. The memo is valid for reconversion for 24 months from the exchange date. Small same-day amounts up to USD 500 equivalent, and up to USD 1,000 equivalent at pre-departure offsite locations, need ID only.
Limit
Bounded by the amount originally exchanged; 24-month validity. ID-only tiers: USD 500 per day, or USD 1,000 per day before departure at designated offsite locations.
Documents required
Valid ID plus the original exchange memo (原兑换水单)
China-side cost
Bank FX spread on the reconversion; Bank of China charges no branch exchange fee (S12)
US tax treatment
Reconversion is a disposition of nonfunctional currency; the IRC 988(e)(2) personal-transaction exclusion applies only where the gain does not exceed USD 200 (S19).

Advantages

  • Clean, expressly authorised route for money you originally brought in and converted
  • Small amounts need ID only

Drawbacks

  • Only works if you kept the original exchange memo
  • Expires 24 months after the original exchange
  • Cannot exceed what was originally converted

Official sources: S01 S12 S19

M07 · Remit RMB offshore under the current account, then convert to USD outside China Lawful if conditions met

Who it is for
Individuals with lawful current-account income such as salary
How it works
Yinfa [2020] No. 330 item 13 supports domestic banks handling individuals' current-account cross-border RMB settlement, expressly facilitating cross-border receipt and payment of salary and other lawful income. The RMB leaves as RMB; conversion to USD happens at the receiving bank offshore.
Limit
—
Documents required
Bank applies the 'three principles of business conduct' (展业三原则) authenticity review. A specific document list for individuals is not set out in item 13.
China-side cost
No onshore CNY-to-USD conversion, so the onshore FX spread is avoided. Offshore conversion spread and bank charges were not verified from an official source.
US tax treatment
Receiving RMB does not avoid US tax. Converting RMB to USD offshore is still a disposition of nonfunctional currency under IRC 988 (S19).

Advantages

  • Avoids the onshore individual FX purchase quota entirely, because no onshore conversion takes place
  • Expressly supported by a six-ministry PBOC notice for salary and other lawful income
  • Offshore conversion is not restricted by SAFE rules once the RMB has lawfully left

Drawbacks

  • Bank must still verify the underlying transaction is genuine and current-account
  • Whether a given bank offers this to a given customer, and any per-transaction cap, could not be confirmed from an official source
  • Does not solve the problem for capital balances such as property sale proceeds

Official sources: S07 S19 F01

M08 · Carry foreign-currency banknotes out of China Lawful if conditions met

Who it is for
Any departing traveller
How it works
Huifa [2003] No. 102 covering notice item 2: up to USD 5,000 equivalent needs no permit; above USD 5,000 up to USD 10,000 needs a Carrying Permit issued by a designated bank; in principle no more than USD 10,000 may be carried out, and only specified special circumstances allow a permit from SAFE above that.
Limit
Per trip: USD 5,000 equivalent permit-free; USD 5,000-10,000 with bank-issued permit; above USD 10,000 in principle not permitted, SAFE permit only for listed special cases. Separate stricter tiers apply to same-day and 15-day repeat crossers.
Documents required
Carrying Permit (携带证) stamped by the bank or SAFE above the permit-free tier
China-side cost
Bank of China lists the FX carrying permit at RMB 10 but waives the fee from 2025-04-01 to 2026-12-31 (S12 item 210101002). Cash conversion spread still applies.
US tax treatment
Entering the US with more than USD 10,000 requires FinCEN Form 105; the threshold aggregates families and groups (S21). Carrying is not itself taxable.

Advantages

  • Immediately available for small amounts without a wire
  • Permit fee currently waived at Bank of China

Drawbacks

  • Effectively capped near USD 10,000 per trip, so useless for a large balance
  • Repeat crossings are pushed into much lower tiers, so repeated runs are not a workaround
  • Carrying in breach is penalised under Order 532 Art. 42 and by Customs
  • Loss or theft risk; US declaration duty on arrival

Official sources: S04 S05 S02 S12 S21

M09 · Carry RMB banknotes out of China Lawful if conditions met

Who it is for
Chinese citizens and foreigners alike
How it works
Customs confirms a limit of RMB 20,000 per person per trip. Amounts above the limit are not released.
Limit
RMB 20,000 per person per trip, excluding same-day and short-period repeat crossers (S05).
Documents required
None within the limit
China-side cost
No conversion occurs in China; RMB must be converted after arrival, at an unverified offshore spread.
US tax treatment
RMB is nonfunctional currency; converting it after arrival is an IRC 988 disposition (S19). Over USD 10,000 equivalent entering the US requires FinCEN 105 (S21).

Advantages

  • Simple for very small amounts

Drawbacks

  • RMB 20,000 is a trivial ceiling
  • RMB is not freely convertible everywhere, so the offshore rate may be worse
  • Excess is not released by Customs

Official sources: S05 S19 S21 F09

  • Practical for living costs rather than capital transfer
  • Published per-transaction fee cap

Drawbacks

  • An annual aggregate overseas cash-withdrawal cap is widely described but could not be confirmed from an official source in this session, so it is left blank and flagged
  • 1% plus a fixed fee is expensive at scale
  • Not a capital repatriation route
