Every way to convert CNY to USD

Seventeen routes, grouped by what they actually are: lawful on ID, lawful on documents, lawful only with SAFE approval, prohibited, and illegal. Each row and card links an official source; anything we could not confirm is left blank and listed on the Flagged page.

Read the status labels carefully

Lawful works on identity documents alone or with routine paperwork. Lawful with documents needs a documented source of funds. Lawful — SAFE approval is a capital-account route with a formal application. Prohibited and Illegal carry penalties. "Conditional — not verified" means we could not confirm the channel from an official source and did not price it.

Route by route

1. Bank wire within the annual quota 境内个人购汇 + 汇出

Lawful
Who it fits: Chinese citizens and foreigners who have lived in China for a full year.

The quota is an allowance, not a right to move unlimited money: within it the bank needs only your ID, but the declared purpose still has to be true.

Limit: US$50,000 per person per calendar year, on ID alone; resets 1 January.
Cost: FX spread + wire fee. $53 on $10,000; $166 on $50,000.

Pros

  • Cheapest lawful route and the least paperwork
  • Done in a branch or banking app; no SAFE approval
  • Creates a clean paper trail for US reporting

Cons

  • Hard annual ceiling per person
  • Purpose must be a genuine current-account purpose — a false purpose is a violation (Art. 12, 44)
  • Bank may ask what the money is for even inside the quota
Source: SAFE Detailed Rules (汇发〔2007〕1号) · SAFE English Q&A (2007) · FX Regulations 2008 (State Council)

2. Bank wire above the quota — documented current-account income 超过年度总额，凭真实性凭证

Lawful with documents
Who it fits: Domestic individuals with genuine income to repatriate: salary, service fees, royalties, family support, donations.

Art. 10 lists the acceptable categories for domestic individuals above the quota: donations, family support, inheritance, insurance, royalties, legal/accounting/consulting services, employee remuneration, overseas investment returns, and 'other' with proof.

Limit: No fixed ceiling; the bank reviews the documents for each transaction.
Cost: Same price as route 1 per transfer: $901 on $400,000 (0.23%).

Pros

  • No dollar cap if the paper is genuine
  • Same low bank cost as the quota route
  • Explicitly contemplated by Art. 10–12 of the Detailed Rules

Cons

  • Document set is demanding and bank-specific
  • Large amounts may be queried or referred to SAFE
  • Salary needs Chinese tax payment records — i.e. the tax must already be settled
Source: SAFE Detailed Rules (汇发〔2007〕1号) · FX Regulations 2008 (State Council) · China Individual Income Tax Law

3. Foreign individual buying FX against documented legal RMB income 境外个人合法人民币收入购汇

Lawful — bank practice decides
Who it fits: A US citizen who does not meet the one-year residence test — including someone who has already left China.

Art. 13(1): a foreign individual's legal current-account RMB income may be purchased into FX on ID plus supporting documents with a transaction amount, including tax certificates. This is the provision a former resident should raise at the bank.

Limit: No dollar ceiling is stated; ID plus documents including the tax certificate.
Cost: Same bank cost as route 1: $901 on $400,000 (0.23%).

Pros

  • The only route in the Detailed Rules that lets a non-resident convert large legal RMB income without the US$50,000 purchase quota
  • Explicit statutory basis (Art. 13(1))
  • Can be paired with property or inheritance documents

Cons

  • Documentary burden is high and branch discretion is real
  • A tax certificate is mandatory — untaxed income cannot be converted this way
  • No published service standard for large amounts; expect delay
Source: SAFE Detailed Rules (汇发〔2007〕1号) · SAFE English Q&A (2007)

4. Remit USD you already hold in a Chinese FX account 外汇储蓄账户汇出

Lawful
Who it fits: Anyone whose account already holds foreign currency rather than RMB.

Art. 14 and 15. If a relative converted the money earlier, the bank will want the conversion trail.

Limit: Domestic individual: up to US$50,000/day on ID. Foreign individual: on ID; cash-funded remittances above US$10,000/day need customs or original withdrawal documents.
Cost: Spread is already sunk; only the wire fee applies (RMB 50–260 + RMB 150).

Pros

  • Cheapest possible transfer if the money is already in USD
  • Daily cap, not annual, for domestic individuals
  • No tax event

Cons

  • Only works for money already converted
  • Cash deposits need source documents
Source: SAFE Detailed Rules (汇发〔2007〕1号)

5. Emigration property transfer 移民转移

Lawful — SAFE approval
Who it fits: A former Chinese national who has moved abroad (or to Hong Kong/Macau) and cancelled their mainland hukou.

PBOC Announcement 2004 No.16. Art. 6 sets the 50% / 25% / 25% sequence; Art. 10 sets the RMB 500,000 approval split; Art. 11 forbids taking the money out as cash.

Limit: No fixed ceiling, but paid out in stages over two years.
Cost: Bank spread and wire fee per instalment plus notarisation/legal costs (unverified). About $901 of bank cost on $400,000.

Pros

  • Purpose-built for taking accumulated wealth out after emigrating
  • Covers the sale proceeds of pre-emigration property
  • Statutory, with published documents and approval thresholds

Cons

  • Requires hukou cancellation and embassy-certified overseas residence proof
  • First payment at most 50%; the rest over two years
  • Above RMB 500,000 the file goes to SAFE headquarters
Source: PBOC 2004 No.16 (property transfer abroad) · SAFE Tianjin Q&A (2026) · SAFE Detailed Rules (汇发〔2007〕1号)

6. Inheritance transfer 继承转移

Lawful — SAFE approval
Who it fits: A foreign citizen (or Hong Kong/Macau resident) who inherited mainland assets.

Art. 9: application, identity and overseas residence proof, proof of inheritance, the deceased's property-rights documents and a tax certificate.

Limit: One application for the whole estate share; may be paid at once or in instalments.
Cost: Bank cost as route 1 plus notarisation/translation (unverified): about $901 at $400,000.

Pros

  • No staging requirement like the emigration route
  • Clear document list in Art. 9
  • Tax clearance is the main gate

Cons

  • Notarised inheritance and property documents required
  • Local SAFE approval; SAFE headquarters above RMB 500,000
  • Cannot be paid in cash
Source: PBOC 2004 No.16 (property transfer abroad) · SAFE Tianjin Q&A (2026)

7. Property-sale proceeds repatriation 转让境内商品房购汇汇出

Lawful — SAFE review
Who it fits: A foreign individual (or foreign branch/representative office) selling a mainland apartment they bought.

汇发〔2006〕47号 Art. 5: purchase application, transfer contract, and the property-transfer tax-clearance certificate.

Limit: Proceeds net of Chinese tax, on approval.
Cost: Bank cost as route 1 plus tax and conveyancing: about $901 of bank cost at $400,000.

Pros

  • Converts a large illiquid asset into a lawful remittance
  • Three-document test is published
  • Pairs naturally with routes 3 and 5

Cons

  • Property-transfer income is taxed at 20% in China
  • Tax clearance must be obtained before SAFE will look at it
  • Local practice and processing time vary
Source: SAFE 汇发〔2006〕47号 (property) · China Individual Income Tax Law

8. Carry foreign-currency cash 携带外币现钞出境

Lawful within limits
Who it fits: Small amounts and travellers.

汇发〔2003〕102号. The bank permit is capped at US$10,000 per certificate, and multiple certificates totalling over US$10,000 are refused at customs.

Limit: Up to US$5,000 without a permit; US$5,000–10,000 with a bank permit; above US$10,000 a SAFE permit.
Cost: Spread only (0.210%) plus travel; not viable at scale — 80 trips for $400,000.

Pros

  • No wire fee
  • Immediate

Cons

  • Tiny per-trip ceiling
  • Physical risk of loss or seizure
  • US$10,000+ into the US must be declared on FinCEN Form 105
Source: SAFE/Customs 汇发〔2003〕102号 (cash) · CBP currency reporting

9. Carry RMB cash 携带人民币现钞

Lawful within limits
Who it fits: Nobody moving meaningful sums.

PBOC Announcement 2004 No.18 sets the RMB 20,000 limit.

Limit: RMB 20,000 per person per trip.
Cost: Exchange it abroad, so the spread depends on the foreign bank; travel costs unverified.

Pros

  • No Chinese conversion paperwork

Cons

  • RMB 20,000 is about US$3,000 — trivial against a six-figure balance
  • Converting RMB outside China can attract an offshore discount
  • Excess cash is fined up to 20% by customs
Source: Shenzhen Port Office on cash limits (2025) · SAFE/Customs 汇发〔2003〕102号 (cash)

10. Spend or withdraw abroad on a domestic bank card 境内银行卡境外使用/取现

Lawful — capped
Who it fits: Living expenses, not capital movement.

汇发〔2017〕29号, effective 2018-01-01.

Limit: Cash withdrawals: RMB 100,000 equivalent per person per calendar year, RMB 10,000 per card per day. Card spending abroad does not use the US$50,000 quota.
Cost: Issuer FX rate plus withdrawal fees (fees unverified).

Pros

  • No quota use for card spending
  • Convenient for living costs

Cons

  • Annual cash ceiling about $14,941
  • Not a way to move a balance
  • Exceeding the cap suspends withdrawals for the rest of the year and the next
Source: SAFE card-cash rule (Xinhua report)

11. QDII funds 合格境内机构投资者

Lawful — investment only
Who it fits: Domestic individuals investing abroad through approved institutions.

Art. 17 permits domestic individuals to invest abroad through banks, fund managers and other qualified domestic institutional investors. It is an investment channel, not a remittance channel.

Limit: Set by the fund's QDII quota, not by you.
Cost: Fund fees and the institution's FX handling (unverified).

Pros

  • Expressly allowed by Art. 17 of the Detailed Rules

Cons

  • You end up with an offshore investment, not USD in your US bank account
  • Redeeming brings value back into the RMB system
  • Not a mechanism for relocating cash
Source: SAFE Detailed Rules (汇发〔2007〕1号)

12. Franchised currency-exchange institutions 个人本外币兑换特许机构

Lawful — small cash amounts
Who it fits: Travellers converting cash.

汇发〔2015〕49号 Art. 6 applies the individual FX rules to franchised exchange institutions.

Limit: Subject to the same individual FX rules and quota system.
Cost: Counter spread (unverified).

Pros

  • Useful for small cash needs at airports and city counters

Cons

  • Cash only
  • Quota still applies
  • Poor rate versus a bank wire
Source: SAFE 汇发〔2015〕49号 (watchlist) · SAFE/Customs 汇发〔2003〕102号 (cash)

13. Western Union / MoneyGram through a bank agent 西联汇款/速汇金

Conditional — availability not verified
Who it fits: Small, fast transfers if the service is offered.

Left unquantified on purpose rather than estimated.

Limit: The underlying conversion is still an individual FX transaction, so the same quota and authenticity rules apply.
Cost: [unverified] — no official outbound CNY→USD tariff located.

Pros

  • Fast payout where available

Cons

  • Fees and FX margin are set by the agent and were not verifiable
  • Availability inside mainland China could not be confirmed from an official source
  • No advantage at six-figure amounts
Source: SAFE Detailed Rules (汇发〔2007〕1号)

14. PayPal / Alipay / WeChat cross-border 第三方支付跨境

Conditional — not a verified route
Who it fits: Small consumer payments.

Included because it is often suggested; the research did not find an official basis for it as a capital-movement channel.

Limit: Not published for outbound personal CNY→USD transfers to a US bank account.
Cost: [unverified]

Pros

  • Familiar interfaces

Cons

  • No official confirmation that a personal outbound CNY→USD account-to-account transfer is supported
  • Would still sit inside China's FX rules if it were
  • Not a route for a balance of any size
Source: SAFE Detailed Rules (汇发〔2007〕1号)

15. Borrowing relatives' quotas / splitting 分拆结售汇、借用额度

Prohibited
Who it fits: Anyone tempted to multiply the US$50,000 by using family members.

汇发〔2015〕49号 Art. 2: no splitting or quota-borrowing to evade the quota or authenticity review. Note that 汇发〔2009〕56号, which listed the 'five or more people' patterns, is repealed and no longer appears in SAFE's current catalogue.

Limit: Explicitly prohibited.
Cost: Watchlist for the year of listing plus two more years; every subsequent FX transaction then needs documents. Fines under the FX Regulations where applicable.

Pros

  • None that survive contact with the rule

Cons

  • The bank's monitoring system flags the pattern
  • Also exposes relatives who lent their quota
  • US gift reporting can still apply
Source: SAFE 汇发〔2015〕49号 (watchlist) · FX Regulations 2008 (State Council)

16. Underground changer / offsetting ('对敲') 地下钱庄、变相买卖外汇

Illegal
Who it fits: Nobody.

Art. 45 covers private trading, disguised trading and broking of foreign exchange; Art. 39 covers evasion.

Limit: No lawful channel exists.
Cost: Fine of up to 30%, or 30%–100% in serious cases, plus confiscation of illegal gains: $120,000–$400,000 at $400,000, versus $901 lawfully.

Pros

  • Fast — and that is the whole of it

Cons

  • Criminal exposure, not just a fine
  • No recourse if the counterparty disappears
  • Contaminated funds can freeze your account and your US bank relationship
Source: FX Regulations 2008 (State Council) · SAFE 汇发〔2015〕49号 (watchlist)

17. Crypto / USDT P2P 虚拟货币

Illegal in China
Who it fits: Nobody.

银发〔2021〕237号 §1(2)–(3).

Limit: Fiat-to-virtual-currency exchange is prohibited.
Cost: No lawful cost basis. Confiscation, account freezing and criminal referral are the realistic outcomes; US capital-gains rules would also apply to any crypto disposal.

Pros

  • None lawful inside mainland China

Cons

  • PBOC's 2021 notice treats fiat/crypto exchange as illegal financial activity
  • Overseas exchanges serving mainland residents are equally unlawful
  • Banks and payment institutions are barred from processing it
Source: PBOC 银发〔2021〕237号 (virtual currency) · FX Regulations 2008 (State Council)

The one thing that decides everything

Whether the bank treats you as a domestic individual (Chinese citizen, or foreigner continuously resident in China for one year) or a foreign individual determines which rule you are arguing under: the US$50,000 purchase quota (domestic) or documented legal income with a tax certificate and no stated ceiling (foreign). Both are in Art. 2 and Art. 13 of the same Detailed Rules. Ask the bank to state which category it has applied to your account, in writing.
