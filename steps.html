Step by step: how each route actually works

Exactly what you do, in order, for each legal channel — and what to do first before you touch any of them.

Step 0 — do this before anything else

None of the routes below can be chosen correctly until you know what the money actually is. Chinese foreign-exchange rules classify the transaction by the source of the funds, not by your passport. Establish: (1) how the money was earned — salary, business income, property sale, gift, inheritance, savings; (2) whether Chinese tax was paid on it and whether you hold a tax certificate (纳税证明); (3) whether you hold, or ever held, a Chinese identity document or a foreign permanent-resident ID card; and (4) whether the money has already been reported to the IRS.

Then ask the branch, in writing, which category it will apply, what documents it needs, and for a written all-in quote. SAFE Detailed Rules

Route A — Documented conversion and a single outward wire

Best when the funds are legitimate current-account income that has borne Chinese tax (for example salary earned while working in China). No annual dollar ceiling appears in the rule text; the constraint is documentation.

Route B — Using the US$50,000 annual quota

Simplest documentation (ID only within the quota) but capped. Note that the quota applies to foreign-exchange purchase by domestic individuals and to settlement; SAFE separately extends it to holders of a foreign permanent-resident ID card. Whether a US passport holder without that card has a quota is not settled by the pages we could verify — confirm with the bank.

Route C — Emigration or inheritance property transfer (SAFE approval)

Route D — Carrying cash out

Only ever sensible for small sums. Above US$10,000 it is in principle not permitted at all.

Do not split cash across people or trips to get around these limits

Splitting to evade the limits, and using underground changers, are the behaviours SAFE publishes enforcement cases about. The statutory range is a fine of 30%–300% of the amount plus confiscation of illegal gains, with criminal liability where a crime is constituted. PRC FX Regulations, Arts. 45–46
