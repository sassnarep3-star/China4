US$50,000/person/yr foreign-exchange quota; ID only within quota. Above quota: evidence of genuine current-account purpose (contracts, invoices). Foreigners employed in China: after-tax salary remittable on top of quota with tax payment records, contract, payslips. FBAR if foreign accounts aggregate >US$10,000; Form 8938/FATCA above thresholds; Form 3520 only if the money is a gift from a foreign person. Legal Official channel; safest; creates paper trail; best exchange rate of the legal options Paperwork above quota; $50,000 annual cap per person without documentation; bank fees SAFE Detailed Rules (Art. 2, 14) · SAFE official site · HSBC China (cites SAFE $50,000 quota)
2. After-tax salary remittance (foreigner employed in China) Remit salary already earned and taxed in China directly abroad. Treated as income repatriation, separate from the $50,000 convenience quota. Amount up to documented after-tax salary. Docs: passport/residence permit, employment contract, individual income tax payment records, salary slips/bank statements. FX spread [unverified] + wire fee [unverified]; China IIT (3–45%) should already have been withheld on the salary Not US taxable if it’s your own already-taxed money. FBAR/Form 8938 reporting may still apply to the account. 3. Carry cash across the border Physically carry USD cash out of China and deposit it in the US. RMB cash: max RMB 20,000. FX cash: ≤US$5,000 no declaration; US$5,001–10,000 needs bank-issued “Carrying Permit”; >US$10,000 needs SAFE approval. Keep the entry declaration to re-export the same cash. None formal; risk of loss/theft; US side: declare >US$10,000 on FinCEN Form 105 at entry Not a taxable event. FinCEN 105 required for >US$10,000 monetary instruments entering the US. FBAR still applies to the account. Legal (within limits) No bank fees; immediate; no conversion paperwork Very small cap; unsafe; declaration duties both countries; impractical for large sums China Customs cash declaration guidance · FinCEN FBAR/currency reporting

Same foreign-exchange controls apply to the underlying CNY→USD conversion ($50,000 annual individual quota unless documented purpose). Limits vary by agent and destination. PayPal-specific limits; underlying FX conversion still subject to China’s individual foreign-exchange rules. No legal channel; evades the $50,000 quota Fine of 30%–300% of the amount, confiscation of illegal gains; criminal liability if a crime is constituted US reporting obligations (FBAR/8938/3520) still apply regardless of channel Illegal None that outweigh the risk Illegal in China; fines 30%–3x the amount; possible criminal prosecution; fraud/counterfeit risk; no recourse Regulations of the PRC on Foreign Exchange Administration (Art. 45/46) · SAFE enforcement cases (fines for illegal FX trading)
