The short answer (best way, least penalty)

Use a normal bank wire (SWIFT) from your Chinese bank, converting CNY to USD within China’s annual individual foreign-exchange quota of US$50,000 per person per calendar year. Within the quota you need only your ID — no tax penalty in China, just the bank’s FX spread and wire fee. Above the quota, you must document the legitimate source of the funds (for a foreigner working in China, after-tax salary with tax payment records can be remitted on top of the quota). Moving your own already-taxed money to the US is not a US taxable event — but you must still file US reports (FBAR if your foreign accounts aggregate over US$10,000; Form 8938/FATCA above higher thresholds; Form 3520 if the money is a gift over US$100,000 from a foreign person). Never use underground money changers or crypto P2P — both are illegal in China and carry fines of 30%–300% of the amount plus criminal risk.

📊 Executive summary

China tightly controls converting renminbi (CNY) into foreign currency and moving it abroad. The key constraint is not a US tax on the transfer itself — it is China’s foreign-exchange quota and the US information-reporting regime. 🖍 Key numbers at a glance

US$50,000 / year

China individual annual foreign-exchange purchase/settlement quota per person (resets 1 Jan). Within quota: ID only at a bank.

US$10,000

FBAR (FinCEN Form 114): file if aggregate foreign accounts exceed $10,000 at any time during the year.

US$100,000

Form 3520: report gifts from foreign individuals/estates above $100,000/yr (gift itself not taxable; reporting is mandatory).

US$5,000

China customs: foreign-currency cash above US$5,000 equivalent must be declared when leaving China; RMB cash cap RMB 20,000.

30%–300%

China fine for illegally buying/selling foreign exchange (underground changers), plus confiscation and possible criminal liability.

No US tax

Transferring your own already-taxed money to the US is not US taxable income. US citizens are taxed on worldwide income — the transfer itself is not an event.
