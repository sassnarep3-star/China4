US tax & reporting obligations

The US does not tax the act of moving your own money across borders. It does demand disclosure of foreign accounts, large foreign gifts and physical cash — with penalties that scale only when filings are missed. All rows verified against IRS / FinCEN / CBP / US Code pages on 2026-11-07.

Sequence that keeps you clean: while the money is still in China you may already pass the FBAR trigger (>$10,000 aggregate) — file it. Convert and wire. The wire into your US bank is not a tax event. Keep Chinese tax records (完税证明) and conversion/wire slips with your US tax file. If the money was really a gift from relatives, check the Form 3520 row below.

Reading the §988 rule on currency conversion

IRC §988(e) does two things for an individual’s personal transactions:

  • §988(e)(1) — the section simply does not apply to “any section 988 transaction entered into by an individual which is a personal transaction”.
  • §988(e)(2) — even when a personal transaction disposes of foreign currency, “no gain shall be recognized … by reason of changes in exchange rates”, unless the gain on that transaction “exceeds $200”.

Practical meaning for this project: converting your own CNY balance into USD is not a taxable event unless, per transaction, currency moves generated a gain above $200 (e.g. you acquired the CNY at a much weaker rate). Document your acquisition dates/rates so the computation is trivial if ever asked.
