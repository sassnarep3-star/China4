The conversion penalty, broken down

What it actually costs to move CNY to USD, channel by channel. China does not charge a “conversion tax” on legal conversions — the penalties come from (a) bank FX spreads and fees, (b) Chinese fines if you use illegal channels, and (c) US reporting penalties if you skip FBAR/Form 8938/Form 3520. Blank cells = not confirmed by an official source.

📋 A. Legal channel costs (bank wire within quota)

Intermediary / correspondent bank fee (US side) Not verified — flagged Deducted by intermediary banks in transit; varies by bank. unverified
China tax on the conversion itself None Converting CNY to USD within the legal quota is not a taxable event in China. Tax only arises if the underlying money is untaxed income (China IIT 3–45%). SAFE Detailed Rules · IRS (worldwide income)
US tax on moving your own money None Transferring your own already-taxed funds to a US account is not a US taxable event. It is a reportable event (FBAR/8938), not a taxable one. IRS Pub 54 · FinCEN FBAR

⚠ B. China-side penalties for illegal channels

Violation Penalty Legal basis Source
Illegally buying/selling foreign exchange (underground changers, 黑庄) Warning + confiscation of illegal gains + fine of 30%–300% of the amount; criminal liability if a crime is constituted Regulations of the PRC on Foreign Exchange Administration, Art. 45/46 Regulations (ChinaMoney mirror) · SAFE enforcement cases
Foreign-exchange evasion (moving funds abroad deceitfully) Ordered to repatriate funds; fine of 30%–500% of the evaded amount; criminal liability if a crime is constituted Regulations, Art. 38/39 Regulations (ChinaMoney mirror)
Carrying foreign currency out of China in violation of customs/FX rules Warning; fine up to 20% of the amount (Customs penalties prevail where applicable) Regulations, Art. 42 Regulations, Art. 42
Individual exceeding the US$50,000 annual quota without documentation Bank cannot process the conversion; must provide evidence of genuine purpose (current account) or SAFE approval (capital account) SAFE Detailed Rules, Art. 2, 10–12 SAFE Detailed Rules

🚪 C. US reporting penalties (failure to file, not tax on the transfer)

Form Trigger Penalty for non-filing Source
FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) Aggregate foreign accounts >US$10,000 at any time during the year Non-willful: up to ~US$16,536 per violation (inflation-adjusted; 2026). Willful: greater of ~US$165,353 or 50% of the account balance per violation FinCEN (how to file FBAR)
Form 8938 (FATCA) Specified foreign financial assets above thresholds (US resident single: >US$50,000 year-end / >US$75,000 any time; higher if living abroad or MFJ) US$10,000 per failure, plus up to US$50,000 continued failure after IRS notice IRS FATCA · IRS Pub 54
Form 3520 (Part IV) Gifts/bequests >US$100,000/yr from foreign individuals or estates (aggregate; related donors combined) 5% of the gift per month late, capped at 25% IRS Form 3520 instructions (PDF) · Form 3520 guide (secondary)
Form 3520 (Parts I–III, foreign trusts) Transactions with foreign trusts (no minimum threshold) Greater of US$10,000 or 35% of the gross reportable amount IRS Form 3520 instructions (PDF)
Underlying foreign income not reported China-source income earned but not declared to IRS Tax owed + accuracy-related penalty (20%, or 40% for undisclosed foreign assets under IRC 6662(j)) + interest IRS FAQ · IRS Pub 54

🛠 Bottom line

The cheapest legal path is a bank wire within the US$50,000 annual quota (cost = bank FX spread + wire fee only; no China tax, no US tax). Above the quota, document after-tax salary/legal source and remit through the bank. The “penalty” is almost entirely avoidable by staying legal — illegal channels charge 30%–300% in fines plus criminal risk, and skipping US reporting can cost tens of thousands in penalties on money that owes no US tax at all.
