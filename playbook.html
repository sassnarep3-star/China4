Step by step

Six lawful routes, written in the order you actually execute them, with the documents that decide each one. Choose the route by your residency status and the source of the money — not by the amount alone.

Before any route

Download or print the last 12 months of statements, and write a one-page source-of-funds memo: when and how the RMB arose, what tax was paid, and what evidence exists. Every route below turns on that memo. Banks do not accept "savings" without a trail.

Route A — Domestic individual, within the US$50,000 quota

Best for: up to US$50,000 a year, per person. Cost ≈ $166 at $50,000. SAFE Detailed Rules (汇发〔2007〕1号) · SAFE English Q&A (2007)

  1. Confirm your categoryAsk the bank in writing whether it treats you as a 境内个人 (Chinese citizen or foreigner continuously resident for one year) or a 境外个人. This decides the rest.
  2. Check the quota you have leftThe annual US$50,000 purchase quota resets on 1 January and is visible in the bank's app or at the counter.
  3. Assemble identity documentsPassport, and either the residence permit/ID used for the account or the entry records.
  4. State a true purposeWithin the quota the bank needs no supporting file, but the declared purpose must be genuine: travel, study, medical, family support, and so on. A false purpose is a violation in its own right.
  5. Convert, then wire — or both at onceThe bank sells you USD at its prevailing rate and sends a SWIFT transfer to your own US account. Take the rate and fee advice in writing.
  6. Keep the paperworkConversion advice, wire confirmation, and the source-of-funds file behind the money.
  7. File the US sideFBAR by 15 April (automatic to 15 October); Form 8938 with the return if above your threshold.

Route B — Domestic individual, above the quota with documents

Best for: salary, service income, family support. No published ceiling. Cost ≈ $901 at $400,000. SAFE Detailed Rules (汇发〔2007〕1号) · FX Regulations 2008 (State Council)

  1. Bring the income fileEmployment contract, payslips and, decisively, the Chinese individual income tax payment records; or the service contract and invoice; or the donation/family-support documents listed in Art. 10.
  2. Ask the bank to process it as an above-quota current-account transactionArt. 10–12 of the Detailed Rules is the basis: ID plus documents showing the transaction amount.
  3. Expect a second-level reviewLarge or unfamiliar files are escalated. Do not split the payment to stay under a threshold — that is the prohibited pattern.
  4. Convert and wire to your own accountThe bank's rate and tariff apply; keep the advice.
  5. File the US sideFBAR and 8938 as applicable; keep the Chinese tax certificate — it supports a foreign tax credit.

Route C — Foreign individual with legal RMB income

Best for: a US citizen who has left China, or never met the one-year residence test. Art. 13(1) route; no dollar ceiling stated; tax certificate mandatory. SAFE Detailed Rules (汇发〔2007〕1号) · SAFE English Q&A (2007)

  1. Frame it as Art. 13, not as the quotaA foreign individual buying FX against legal RMB income: ID plus documents with a transaction amount, including the tax certificate. No dollar ceiling is stated in the rule.
  2. Prove the RMB is legal incomeSalary with tax receipts; sale proceeds with the transfer contract and tax clearance; inheritance with the estate documents; service income with the contract and invoice.
  3. Get the tax certificate firstWithout it the conversion has no basis. If the income was never taxed, settle that with the Chinese tax authority before approaching the bank.
  4. Submit for authenticity reviewThe bank reviews and may refer the file to the local SAFE branch. Ask for the decision in writing.
  5. Wire to your own account abroadAsk the bank to remit to an account in your own name. Where the property-transfer rules apply, Art. 11 of PBOC 2004 No.16 requires the bank to remit the funds and forbids handing them over as cash.
  6. File the US sideFBAR, 8938 as applicable; report the income if it was not reported before.

Route D — Emigration property transfer (移民转移)

Best for: a former Chinese national with a six-figure balance and cancelled hukou. Staged over two years. PBOC 2004 No.16 (property transfer abroad) · SAFE Tianjin Q&A (2026)

  1. Check eligibilityYou moved from mainland China to a foreign country (or Hong Kong/Macau) and your mainland hukou has been cancelled; you are transferring property you owned before acquiring that emigration status.
  2. Assemble the file for the SAFE branch where your hukou wasWritten application; the emigration-transfer applicant form; the FX business application form; hukou cancellation certificate plus embassy/consulate-certified overseas residence proof; property-rights documents; and the tax certificate or tax-payment proof.
  3. Apply once for the whole amountArt. 6 requires a single application covering all the property you intend to move.
  4. Obtain approvalUp to RMB 500,000: the local SAFE branch decides. Above that: local branch screens, SAFE headquarters approves. You then receive an approval letter and a registration certificate for the bank.
  5. First remittance: no more than 50%Present the approval documents to the designated bank; the bank converts and wires to your own account abroad.
  6. Second stage after one year, third after twoEach later remittance needs the prior approval letter and the previous registration certificate.
  7. File the US sideFBAR/8938 for the year of each remittance; keep every SAFE document — it is the cleanest possible source-of-funds record.

Route E — Inheritance transfer (继承转移)

Best for: inherited mainland assets. PBOC 2004 No.16 (property transfer abroad)

  1. Prove the inheritanceNotarised will/estate documents or the court's inheritance determination, plus your identity and, for a foreign citizen, embassy/consulate-certified overseas residence proof.
  2. Prove the assets and the tax positionThe deceased's property-rights documents and the tax certificate from the tax authority where the assets are.
  3. Apply at the SAFE branch where the deceased's hukou wasOne application for everything inherited from that person; different deceased persons require separate applications.
  4. Obtain approval and remitOne payment or several — the rule allows either for inheritance, unlike the emigration route.
  5. File the US sideForm 3520 if the bequest exceeds US$100,000 from that foreign estate.

Route F — Property-sale proceeds

Best for: an apartment sold by a foreign individual. SAFE 汇发〔2006〕47号 (property)

  1. Sell and pay the Chinese taxProperty-transfer income is taxed at 20% in China; obtain the tax-clearance certificate.
  2. Apply to the local SAFE branchPurchase application, the property transfer contract, and the tax-clearance certificate for the transfer (汇发〔2006〕47号 Art. 5).
  3. Convert and wireAfter SAFE review the bank converts the RMB proceeds and remits them.
  4. File the US sideReport the capital gain, claim the foreign tax credit, and file FBAR/8938 as applicable.

Small amounts only — cash and cards

  • FX cash out of China: up to US$5,000 with no permit; US$5,000–10,000 with a bank-issued permit; above US$10,000 a SAFE permit. Declare anything over US$5,000 when bringing cash into China. SAFE/Customs 汇发〔2003〕102号 (cash)
  • RMB cash: RMB 20,000 per person per trip. Shenzhen Port Office on cash limits (2025)
  • Into the US: over US$10,000 in currency or monetary instruments must be reported on FinCEN Form 105, and the threshold applies to a family travelling together collectively. CBP currency reporting
  • Card withdrawals abroad: RMB 100,000 per person per calendar year; RMB 10,000 per card per day. SAFE card-cash rule (Xinhua report)
