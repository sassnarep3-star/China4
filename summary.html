Executive answer

For a US citizen holding CNY in a Chinese bank account, the best-evidenced route is a licensed-bank conversion and an own-account international wire supported by lawful source-of-funds and Chinese tax evidence. The USD 50,000 annual convenience purchase quota is written for domestic individuals; it is not blanket ID-only permission for a foreign individual. Moving your own principal is not itself new taxable income, but it is not proof of zero tax: underlying income, interest, currency gains, tax residence and US information returns must be analyzed separately. 04 · Execution

Eight steps, in order

  1. Classify your identity with the bank: SAFE defines a passport-holding foreign citizen as a foreign individual, which is not the same as a domestic individual for the annual convenience quota. [S2] [S1]
  2. Classify the money: after-tax salary, existing foreign currency, unused RMB from an earlier conversion, inheritance and property proceeds each follow different rules. [S1] [S20] [S21]
  3. Assemble the evidence chain: passport, contracts/payslips, amount-matched records, Chinese tax receipts and prior US returns. [S1] [S32]
  4. Resolve US reporting first: FBAR, Form 8938, Form 3520 and any past-due filings are independent of the transfer rail. [S25] [S27] [S28] [S36]
  5. Ask the Chinese bank for a written all-in quote: CNY debit, FX rate, commission, telecom, correspondent deduction and final USD credit. [S4] [S6]
  6. If eligible, obtain Wise's live CNY quote and compare the final USD received — not just the headline fee. [S11] [S13]
  7. Execute the documented route to your own US account; never split to evade review or borrow another person's quota. [S1] [S19]
  8. Keep the FX memo, all fee receipts and the final credit; reconcile against the model and update FBAR maximum balances. [S4] [S25]
