Tax conclusion

Moving your own existing bank balance is not, by itself, a new wage or gift. That does not establish ‘zero tax’: underlying income/gains, interest, personal FX gain, tax residence and information returns still require analysis. No personalized tax amount can be calculated from principal alone.

China underlying tax

Resident comprehensive-income schedule

China’s IIT law makes a person domiciled in China, or without domicile but present at least 183 days in a tax year, a resident individual. Residents are taxed on China and foreign-source income; nonresidents on China-source income. The table applies to annual taxable comprehensive income after statutory deductions—not gross remittance principal. Nonresidents generally calculate wages monthly using the monthly-converted schedule and a ¥5,000 monthly expense deduction. Non-domicile 90-day, 183-day, six-year and treaty rules can change the income included. [S39] [S40]

Why the measured-gain column is blank

A currency gain is measured between the rate when the CNY was acquired and the rate when it is converted. Acquisition dates and rates for a pre-existing balance are personal facts no public source can supply, so none is inferred. Publication 525 excludes a personal foreign-currency transaction gain unless it is more than $200 and reports it as a capital gain above that amount, so a held-for-personal-use balance converted once is a small separate line — not a percentage levy on the principal. Execution worksheet

Ten questions for a cross-border tax professional

  1. Which years did the CNY income arise, and what were your China/US tax-residence positions in each?
  2. How much is principal, interest, capital gain, gift, inheritance or business equity?
  3. Which Chinese taxes were withheld or paid, and do you have amount-matched official records?
  4. Which income was already reported on US returns, and at what translated USD basis?
  5. Does FEIE qualify, and would FTC produce a better result across current and carryover years?
  6. Are you claiming any treaty position requiring specific disclosure or residency analysis?
  7. Does personal FX gain exceed $200, or do business/investment FX rules apply instead?
  8. What were each foreign account’s maximum values for every open FBAR year?
  9. Do Form 8938, 3520, trust/entity, state or other information returns apply independently?
  10. If past filings were incomplete, are you eligible for streamlined or another formal correction procedure?

Personal tax due, China exit-related professional costs and US tax on the transfer are intentionally blank until these facts are known. See Flagged for Review.
